How EMI is calculated

EMI means equated monthly installment. For a fixed-rate loan, each payment covers the interest due for that month and reduces the principal. At the start, the outstanding balance is larger, so the interest portion is usually larger. As principal is repaid, the interest portion generally falls. The monthly installment is calculated from the principal, monthly interest rate and number of payments.

The formula is EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the annual rate divided by 1,200, and n is the number of monthly installments. If the interest rate is zero, the loan amount is divided evenly across the term. The displayed installment is rounded to the nearest cent. Each month's interest is then rounded to cents, and the final payment is adjusted to close the balance. For example, on PKR 1,000,000 at 18% annual interest for 36 months, the formula gives an unrounded monthly EMI of about PKR 36,152.3955, displayed as PKR 36,152.40 (PKR 36,152 to the nearest rupee). The formula-based total is PKR 1,301,486.24 and formula-based interest is PKR 301,486.24. If all 36 monthly installments were instead paid at the rounded PKR 36,152.40, they would sum to PKR 1,301,486.40. The PKR 0.16 difference arises from rounding; this calculator adjusts the final installment and rounds monthly interest to cents, so its actual amortization schedule may differ slightly from either illustrative total. Check the displayed schedule for the actual total.

Worked example: for a loan of Rs 1,000,000 at an annual rate of 12% over 12 months, the monthly rate is 1%. The calculated EMI is Rs 88,848.79. Under this calculator's monthly-cent rounding rules, total interest is Rs 66,185.45 and total payments are Rs 1,066,185.45. A bank can show a slightly different result if it uses different day-count conventions, fees or rounding rules, so compare its official repayment schedule before agreeing to a loan.

How to lower your EMI

A longer term usually lowers the required monthly payment because the balance is spread across more months, but it often increases total interest. A lower annual rate can reduce both the monthly payment and the interest cost. Compare offers using the same principal, term, fees and rate type so that the comparison is meaningful. Ask whether a quoted rate is fixed or variable and whether the installment can change later.

You can also consider borrowing a smaller amount, making a larger down payment, or paying down high-cost debt before taking a new loan. Do not choose a payment only because it fits the first month. Keep room in your budget for essential costs, irregular bills and emergencies. A low EMI over a long period may cost more overall than a higher payment over a shorter period.

How prepayment saves interest

An extra payment reduces the principal that future interest is calculated on. If you keep the EMI unchanged, the loan may finish earlier and future interest can fall. If you keep the original end date instead, the EMI can be recalculated using the lower balance and the remaining months. The prepayment planner models both choices and caps an extra payment at the outstanding balance.

Actual savings depend on when the lender credits the extra payment, whether fees apply, and whether the loan permits partial prepayment. Some lenders apply charges or require a minimum extra payment. Confirm the lender's rules and ask for an updated schedule before transferring money. Treat the result here as a scenario, not a promise of savings.

Checklist before taking a loan

Check the annual rate, whether it is fixed or variable, the total amount repayable, processing fees, insurance, late charges and early repayment rules. Request a written schedule showing each installment's principal and interest. Check whether the lender's quoted EMI includes all mandatory charges or whether some costs are collected separately.

Test whether the payment remains manageable if income drops or other expenses rise. Compare offers over the same term and currency, and check the total interest rather than looking only at the monthly installment. Read the contract and ask questions about any amount or condition you do not understand. Do not share passwords, one-time codes or unnecessary personal information with anyone claiming to arrange a loan.

Important limitations

This is a general-purpose estimate for fixed-rate, monthly-repayment loans. It does not model taxes, insurance, fees, variable rates, irregular payment dates or every lender's rounding method unless those amounts are included in the principal. It is not financial advice, a quote or an approval decision. Confirm the terms with your lender before making a commitment.

Frequently asked questions

What does EMI mean?

EMI means equated monthly installment. It is the regular amount paid toward a loan each month, including principal and interest, except that the final payment may be adjusted to clear the remaining balance.

Is the EMI guaranteed by a bank?

No. This calculator provides an estimate from the amount, annual rate and term you enter. A lender may use different fees, rates, day-count rules or rounding methods.

Does the interest rate mean annual interest?

Yes. Enter the annual nominal rate as a percentage. The calculator divides it by twelve to estimate the monthly rate and does not include fees unless you add them to the loan amount.

Can I repay the loan early?

Use the prepayment section to model a one-time extra payment or a recurring monthly extra payment. Actual lender rules, fees and payment dates can change the savings.

Why can my bank EMI differ?

Banks may apply processing fees, insurance, different compounding or day-count conventions, payment dates and rounding rules. Compare the lender's written amortization schedule before signing.

Is the affordability result a loan approval?

No. It is a planning estimate based on your income, existing EMIs and chosen limit. A lender separately checks credit history, income stability, expenses and its own eligibility rules.

Use this calculator as a planning aid. Check every rate, fee and repayment condition in your lender's documents before accepting a loan.
